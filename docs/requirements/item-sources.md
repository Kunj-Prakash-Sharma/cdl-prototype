## Licence check
- **IPIP (personality):** read by the team on 2026-10-30 at [ipip.ori.org/newPermission.htm](https://ipip.ori.org/newPermission.htm): "Because the IPIP has been placed in the public domain, permission has already been automatically granted ... for any person to use IPIP items, scales, and inventories for any purpose, commercial or non-commercial." No attribution requested.
- **ORVIS (interests), confirmed 2026-11-03:** the [IPIP home page](https://ipip.ori.org/) says "The items and scales are in the public domain, which means that one can copy, edit, translate, or use them for any purpose without asking permission and without paying a fee." It does not name ORVIS, and the ORVIS key page has no licence text. The paper that introduces it, [Pozzebon, Visser, Ashton, Lee & Goldberg (2010), PMC2822996](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2822996), was read in full by the team on 2026-11-03 and says so repeatedly: title "a Public-Domain Self-Report Measure of Vocational Interests"; abstract "a brief public-domain alternative to commercial inventories"; introduction "available in the public domain"; discussion "the most thoroughly validated measure of vocational interests available in the public domain". The items were generated by Goldberg from IPIP and BRI items; the commercial CISS was used only as conceptual guidance, not copied.
- **Verdict: free to use, no licence needed.** Residual risk is very small (the CISS point above); the client's lawyer can look at it together with the footer wording if the client wants it in writing. This is the team's reading of public pages, not legal advice. The paper's "Copyright and License information" panel covers the article text, not the items; we cite the paper and do not copy it.
- **Holland mapping (from the paper's appendix):** Leadership = Enterprising, Organization = Conventional, Altruism = Social, Creativity = Artistic, Analysis = Investigative, Production and Adventure = Realistic (the paper calls them a split of Realistic). Erudition has no Holland counterpart and is not used. The paper calls the scales "similar in content" to Holland's types and validates them against the CISS, not against Holland's own tools, so say "based on Holland's six interest types", never "validated".
- **Limits to remember:** reliabilities in the paper (.75 to .91) belong to the original items, not our rewrites. Samples were an older Oregon community sample (mean age 62) and Canadian first-year college students, not Indian teenagers. Raw averages differ by scale (about 3.2 for Creativity, Altruism and Production against about 2.5 for Analysis and Adventure) and men scored much higher on Adventure and Analysis, so ranking raw scores can favour types people rate higher on average. We use no percentiles; if the top-3 code looks skewed in real use, revisit the ranking in a new set version.

`Match`: **direct** = same idea, near same words. **close** = same idea, different words.

## Rules for IDs and changes
- **IDs freeze when a real learner's answer is first stored** (launch, or staging with real users). Until then draft sets may be re-numbered freely. After that an ID never changes meaning; a changed idea gets a new ID and a new set version.
- Rewording an item but keeping its idea: patch version (1.0.0 to 1.0.1).
- Adding, removing or re-keying items, or changing the quiz length: new set version (1.1.0). Old attempts keep the version they started with.
- Never add an item without a `source` (a test enforces it).
- O*NET Mini-IP is a possible later upgrade for interests (licence unclear; see `assessment.md` section 9). No O*NET wording is used.
